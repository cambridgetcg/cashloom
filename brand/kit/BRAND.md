# CashLoom: brand kit

> Make a money plan, keep your records together, and understand your options. Practical tools for individuals, households, independent work, and shared projects.

Generated by `artcraft-brand brandkit.py 1.8` with vectorcraft-cli 0.17.0, from `source/brand.json`. Every vector and raster was drawn by VectorCraft in one headless session (`brand.vectorcraft` is that document); stdlib Python did the colour maths, the ICO packing and these documents. Open `index.html` to review everything on light and dark grounds.

## Logo

**Mark:** monogram "c" in Source Serif 4 Regular, in a rounded square. **Wordmark:** "CashLoom" in Inter SemiBold, tracking -33/1000 em, outlined. Every SVG is outlined paths with no `<text>`, no strokes and named ids.

Proportions are derived from the mark, so they hold at any size:

- **Horizontal lockup:** wordmark cap height = 0.50 x mark height; mark-to-word gap = 0.30 x mark height. The wordmark is centred on the mark by its cap height (the band from baseline to the top of a capital H), not by its bounding box, so descenders and lowercase letters do not pull it off centre.
- **Stacked lockup:** cap height = 0.32 x mark height; gap from the mark to the top of the capitals = 0.21 x mark height; both centred on one axis.

**Clear space.** Keep a margin of at least **x** on every side of any logo, where **x is the wordmark's cap height** (the height of a capital H as set in the lockup). For the horizontal lockup x = 0.50 x the mark's height (48 px at the 1x size, 96 px tall). For the mark alone, use x = a quarter of the mark's height. Nothing else (text, edges, other logos) enters that space.

**Minimum sizes** (below these the wordmark loses its letterforms; use the mark alone):

| asset | screen | print |
|---|---|---|
| wordmark | 80 px wide (cap height 12 px) | 20 mm wide (cap height 3 mm) |
| horizontal lockup | 120 px wide (cap height 12 px) | 28 mm wide (cap height 3 mm) |
| stacked lockup | 80 px wide (cap height 12 px) | 20 mm wide (cap height 3 mm) |
| mark | 16 px (below 24 px use the hinted favicon cuts) | 6 mm |

**Colour versions.** Full colour on light grounds. The `-reversed` lockups and `wordmark-reversed` are for dark grounds: the wordmark is white, and the badge #255B46 moves to #5E937C (primary 500, the dark-mode UI colour, 3.52:1 on #183A30) and its letters take #181F1B, 4.75:1 on it; #FFFFFF becomes #181F1B. On dark grounds a mark colour moves up its own scale until it reaches 3:1 and APCA Lc 30 (the dark-mode UI bar). A badge that holds letters takes that same UI step: with its letters when they keep 4.5:1 on it, else with its letters cut out when the ground shows through them at 4.5:1, else with its letters in the ink that reads on it; only when none of these works does it take the step that keeps its letters at 4.5:1. White only when no step does. In the one-colour marks, the letter is cut out of the badge, so the mark keeps its form in one ink.

**Do not:** recolour outside the palette, stretch, rotate, add effects or outlines, set the name in another font, rebuild the lockup by hand, or place the full-colour logo on a ground close to its own colours (a fill of the brand colour, a busy photo): use the one-colour or reversed versions there.

Hex is the source value (sRGB). OKLCH is shown for reference and drives the scales; it is never written as a fill in any vector file. Contrast is WCAG 2.2 (the conformance bar); APCA Lc is in `colour/contrast.md` as guidance.

| accent | `#648045` | `oklch(56.3% 0.091 129.7)` | 100 128 69 | C58 M21 Y80 K19 | 4.45 | 4.72 |
| neutral | `#57685E` | `oklch(50.0% 0.026 159.5)` | 87 104 94 | C53 M26 Y45 K36 | 5.92 | 3.55 |
| light ground | `#F7F8F2` | `oklch(97.7% 0.008 114.2)` | 247 248 242 | C4 M1 Y5 K0 | 1.07 | 19.66 |
| dark ground | `#183A30` | `oklch(31.9% 0.044 171.8)` | 24 58 48 | C68 M21 Y55 K68 | 12.45 | 1.69 |

CMYK is VectorCraft's conversion (`color.convert`) into its default CMYK working space, rounded to whole percent: a starting point for a printer, not a proof. Ask for a proof on the actual stock before a print run; spot colours are not chosen here.

Each colour has an 11-step OKLCH scale (50 to 950) in `colour/tokens.css`, `tokens.json` and the swatch libraries; the brand colour itself is the step nearest its lightness.

**Roles** (what the tokens are for). Each role is the step of its scale nearest the brand colour that reaches its WCAG 2.2 bar and an APCA floor: |Lc| 45 for text (`text-muted`, `*-text`) and 30 for UI (`*-ui`, `focus`), because WCAG 2 over-rates light colours on dark grounds. So on dark grounds the logo's lifted colour (#5E937C) is the dark-mode UI step: logo, focus rings and controls share one dark primary. In dark mode --primary is #5E937C (primary 500, 3.52:1 on the dark ground), not the brand colour #255B46, which is 1.58:1 there and would disappear as a fill; light mode keeps it. `--surface` and `--border` are derived from the light and dark grounds you gave: the same lightness steps away from them as the neutral 100/200 (light) and 900/800 (dark) steps sit from the default grounds, in the ground's own hue, so cards and rules show on it. `on-*` is the ink on a brand fill: the brand's dark text colour or white, whichever reads better at 4.5:1, the same ink the logo uses.

**In code.** `colour/tokens.css` defines `--<scale>-<step>` and the roles (`--bg`, `--surface`, `--text`, `--primary` …) for light and dark mode. For Tailwind CSS v4, `@import "./colour/tailwind.theme.css";` after `@import "tailwindcss";` gives every step as a utility (`bg-primary-600`, `text-neutral-700`) and the roles as utilities that follow the mode (`bg-bg`, `bg-surface`, `text-text-muted`, `border-border`, `ring-focus`, `bg-primary` with `text-on-primary`), plus `font-display`, `font-body` and `font-mono`. For Tailwind v3, `theme.extend.colors = require("./colour/tailwind.colors.js")` gives the same class names; load `tokens.css` too, because the roles there are `var(--…)`. The brand's neutral scale replaces Tailwind's own `neutral`.

## Icons

- **App icons** (`apple-touch-icon`, `icon-192`, `icon-512`): full-bleed #255B46 tile, the letter alone in #DCEBB9 (as given in icon.ink); the tile takes the place of the badge. The mark spans 60% of the tile (measured; icon.padding 0.2 a side), centred on the ink both ways (a lowercase letter does not fill a cap height). No rounded corners are baked in: iOS and Android apply their own masks.
- **Maskable** (`icon-maskable-512`): the same as the app icon; its farthest pixel, 159 px from the centre, is already inside the 204.8 px safe circle that Android masks keep.
- **Favicons:** #255B46 tile, 28% corner radius. Each size has its own cut:

  | size | cut | mark size | margins (left, top, right, bottom), measured | clean pixels, predicted |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 16 px | the monogram, x-height 11.4 px | 12 px | 3, 2, 3, 2 | 64% |
  | 32 px | the monogram, x-height 24.7 px | 26 px | 5, 3, 5, 3 | 71% |
  | 48 px | the monogram, x-height 36 px | 38 px | 8, 5, 8, 5 | 82% |

  Each cut is hinted before it is drawn. Its box is set on whole pixels; every cut is centred to the pixel. Stems and bars that are straight, or within half a pixel of straight (a serif face's slightly flared stems, the longest straight part of a stem that flows into its bracket, and three quarters of a pixel for a long straight edge), are made straight, and their edges and every curve's outermost point move to whole pixels, shape by shape, so those strokes render as solid pixels rather than half-tones; diagonals and curves stay anti-aliased. Points two shapes share (a common vertex or edge) are welded to one position, so no crack of the ground opens where they meet (verify checks the pixels deep inside the ink for it), and where hinting closes a thin gap between two shapes of one colour, the speck it would leave at the gap's end is filled. A band that holds a stroke keeps at least one pixel however thin, so hinting never deletes an arm or a bar (verify compares every cut with its unhinted outline to prove it); a stem keeps its own width rounded, so equal stems stay equal; edges that nearly meet share a pixel line, so a counter never notches the stem beside it; a counter keeps a pixel only where it separates strokes that face each other. A shape that is mirror-symmetric about its box is hinted on one side and mirrored, and two shapes that mirror each other share one hinting, so a symmetric mark stays symmetric; when most of a mark mirrors about an axis of its own, contour by contour (a symmetric body beside an ornament off its axis), that part is hinted about its axis set on a pixel edge or centre, and the rest beside it (verify checks the pixels of both cases). On a tile, the cut is made 2 px smaller until no ink pixel touches the tile's soft rounded corner. `favicon.svg` carries the 32 and 16 px cuts and shows the 16 px one when drawn under 24 px; `favicon.ico` holds the 16, 32 and 48 px renders.

**Tabs.** The tile #255B46 against Chrome's tab colours: white (light, active tab) 7.88:1 (OKLab dE 0.58), #DEE1E6 (light, tab strip) 6.01:1 (OKLab dE 0.49), #202124 (dark, tab strip) 2.04:1 (OKLab dE 0.19), #35363A (dark, active tab) 1.53:1 (OKLab dE 0.12). It stands apart from every tab colour by lightness.

## Website head

Copy the files in `web/` to the site root and put this early in `<head>` of every page (from `web/head-snippet.html`). It is the minimal modern set; obsolete tags (`shortcut icon`, `mask-icon`, `msapplication-*`, extra apple-touch sizes) are deliberately left out. Its `color-scheme` meta says `light` (brand.json `app.color_scheme`): set it to the schemes the site's pages really support, and leave it out where the framework writes one.

```html
<!-- Icons: three links and the manifest. Serve the files in web/ at the site root. -->
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon.ico" sizes="32x32">
<link rel="icon" href="/favicon.svg" type="image/svg+xml">
<link rel="apple-touch-icon" href="/apple-touch-icon.png">
<link rel="manifest" href="/site.webmanifest">
<meta name="theme-color" content="#F7F8F2">

<!-- Colour schemes this page supports (brand.json app.color_scheme). "light dark" is true when the page loads colour/tokens.css or has its own dark theme. Light-only page: "only light" (plain "light" lets browsers auto-darken it). Dark-only: "dark" ("only dark" is invalid). One per page: leave this out if your framework writes one (Next.js viewport.colorScheme). -->
<meta name="color-scheme" content="light">

<!-- Social preview: keep these early in <head>, within the first 300 KB of the HTML (WhatsApp). og:title should be the page title, not the site name; pages with their own card have their lines in og/head-<slug>.html. -->
<meta property="og:type" content="website">
<meta property="og:site_name" content="CashLoom">
<meta property="og:title" content="Your money. Your direction.">
<meta property="og:locale" content="en_US">
<meta property="og:description" content="Make a money plan, keep your records together, and understand your options.">

<meta property="og:image" content="https://cashloom.io/og.jpg">

<meta property="og:image:width" content="1200">
```

`web/site.webmanifest` sets `"display": "browser"`, `theme_color` #F7F8F2 and `background_color` #F7F8F2 (from brand.json "app"; the default is a standalone app on the light ground). Its `lang` is en.

On the home page only, add `web/head-home.html`: schema.org `Organization` and `WebSite` blocks for Google Search. The logo is `https://cashloom.io/icon-512.png`, the square app icon (512 px, opaque, so it looks right on white as Google asks); keep that file at that URL. **Social card** (`web/og.jpg`): light theme, title "Your money. Your direction." at 84 px in 2 lines (15.72:1), the subtitle at 30 px in Light, the mark at the right, kept inside the centred 1200 x 600 band that X crops to. Every line is measured and set inside its column, clear of the mark and the footer (`og.text_fits` checks the pixels too); lines break at spaces, preferring the end of a sentence, a colon, a dash or a comma. Baseline JPEG, one interleaved scan, sRGB profile. Change the image URL when the card changes: platforms cache by URL. Its `og:image:alt` describes the picture: "CashLoom card: the “c” monogram in a dark teal rounded square beside the headline “Your money. Your direction.” on a very light grey background."

Notes from the research, untested on devices: iOS 26 Safari reportedly ignores `theme-color` and tints from the page background instead (WebKit bug 301756), while Chrome on Android and installed apps still use it. A social card cannot follow light or dark mode (`og:image` has no media query); for a README or a hero image that should, use `<picture>` with `logo/lockup-horizontal.svg` and a `prefers-color-scheme: dark` source of `lockup-horizontal-reversed.svg`. Apple's Messages guidance (TN3156) says to avoid text in preview images; this card carries the title because X shows a link's headline only as a small overlay on the image, if at all (research, 2024), a trade-off to know. A `monochrome` manifest icon is optional and its support unconfirmed; `logo/mark-mono-black.svg` could serve as one.

## What this kit does not make

Recipes are in `~/.kingdom/tools/artcraft-brand/references/recipes.md`; each needs its own checks.

- Social banners and headers (X header, LinkedIn cover, YouTube channel art, Facebook cover): not covered; each platform's sizes and the area its avatar covers need checking first. FigCraft `data.fill` (0.11.2+, recipes.md §10) makes post images at several sizes from one template.
- A print guideline PDF: recipes.md §3 (DesignCraft one-pager). `index.html` prints to a usable sheet (print styles keep its dark grounds).
- Business cards with bleed and crop marks: recipes.md §4 (DesignCraft).
- CMYK separations, spot colours and press proofs: the CMYK above is an approximate conversion only.
- A BIMI logo for email inboxes: recipes.md §9 lists its prerequisites (SVG Tiny PS, a mark certificate, DMARC enforcement); not a recipe.
- Figma or FigCraft variables: recipes.md §5 (FigCraft colour variables, with guards).
- More page cards than the 40 that "pages" takes, or cards from a CMS: recipes.md §1 (DesignCraft data merge) and §2 (PhotoCraft data sets).

## Rebuild and verify

Edit `source/brand.json` and run, from inside this folder:

```sh
python3 ~/.kingdom/tools/artcraft-brand/scripts/brandkit.py source/brand.json --out . --rebuild-check
python3 ~/.kingdom/tools/artcraft-brand/scripts/verify.py .        # re-run every check; rewrites verify.json and the section below
```

The build happens in a sibling staging folder and replaces the kit's files only when it finishes, so a failed build leaves this kit as it was. It is deterministic: rebuilding gives byte-identical files, the two PDFs included. Their creation date is 2026-10-27 09:43:33 UTC (`source_date_epoch` in kit.json): the kit's first build time, kept across in-place rebuilds, unless the build was given SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH. Set SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH (whole seconds since 1970) when building to choose another. `--rebuild-check` proves it each time by building again from `source/brand.json` in a temp folder and comparing every file byte for byte.

## What was not checked

- Rendering in real browsers: tab favicons, the favicon.svg size switch and dark-scheme rule, and how a browser chooses between favicon.ico and favicon.svg were not observed.
- Home-screen and launcher masks on real iOS and Android devices; only the 40% safe circle was measured.
- Social previews on live platforms (X, LinkedIn, Slack, iMessage): only file size, dimensions, tags, the JPEG scan layout and a decode by djpeg or sips were checked. Post a test link once before relying on the card.
- Print: PDF colour stays RGB; the CMYK values in BRAND.md are VectorCraft's conversion, not a proof; no bleed or spot colours.
- Font licences beyond what BRAND.md "Typefaces and licences" states: faces bundled with vectorcraft-cli are OFL-1.1; the licence of any other face was not checked.
- Email clients (the on-light PNG in a signature: dark modes, scaling), platforms' avatar crops, and whether Google uses the Organization logo were not observed; the Tailwind files were checked as text against palette.json, not built with Tailwind.
- APCA contrast is guidance (WCAG 3 drafts); the colour roles use it as a floor, but WCAG 2.2 is the bar that was checked for conformance.
- The design itself: the kit reproduces the monogram and wordmark as set in the font; it does not judge originality, trademark clearance or cultural reading.
